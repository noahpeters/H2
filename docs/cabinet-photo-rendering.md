# Room surfaces and Take Photo

Room controls select three wall treatments (warm plaster, white plaster, sage painted plaster), three floor treatments (light wood planks, dark stained planks, smooth concrete), and three countertop treatments (white quartz, Taj Mahal, dark granite). Existing `room.walls` and `room.floor` IDs are retained, including legacy oak/walnut IDs; the plank scan does not identify its wood species. Optional `room.countertopMaterial` persists through existing room JSON save/load, undo/redo and sharing; older rooms default to white quartz. Server validation rejects unknown countertop IDs without changing the room protocol version.

`roomMaterials.ts` uses the existing `MaterialDefinition`, `createMaterial`, immutable texture cache, load/fallback handling and metre-based UV projection. Geometry factories still produce the same stock, openings, overhangs and sink cutouts. `StudyScene` assigns room PBR materials to floors, walls, cabinet countertops, dishwasher countertops and island tops. Cache signatures include countertop selection. Walls retain their existing transparency and floors retain double-sided rendering. Material transitions wait for texture loading and preserve the previous scene until replacement is ready.

The Taj Mahal control includes a visible representative-preview note. An exact slab scan can replace this explicit definition without changing the rendering architecture. Finish/IOR/tint values are visual preview assumptions, not laboratory measurements. CC0 1K source maps are bundled under `public/textures/room`, with provenance, source download URLs, authors where supplied, source footprint information and SHA-256 hashes. Poly Haven sets include albedo, OpenGL normal, roughness and AO; granite includes albedo, OpenGL normal and roughness. Source maps are unmodified. No displacement or AI-generated textures are used.

## Photo lifecycle

The interactive Three.js renderer remains mounted and demand-rendered with its existing camera, controls, geometry and renderer configuration. `Take Photo` synchronously captures a private scene/camera copy, including current door/drawer poses, transforms, visibility, FOV, aspect, zoom, material definitions and grain mapping. Capture waits until the live scene has finished its current material transition. Geometry and materials belong to the copy; texture resources are retained through the existing reference-counted cache. Later design edits and camera navigation cannot modify captured pixels.

`photoRender.ts` is a separate on-demand Three.js render pipeline and GPU lifetime. It eases rectangular cabinet stock by 1mm and countertops by 2mm. Box envelopes stay exact; extrusions inset their end caps and retain their central outline, thickness and holes. Shaker/slatted custom front pieces retain independent grain axes and glass material groups. Profile-deformed stock is kept intact rather than replaced by rectangular approximations. Unsupported geometry shapes retain their exact current mesh; they can acquire additional photo treatments later. All enhancement geometry is created in the photo copy and never enters saved designs, placement, dimensions, snapping, takeoffs or fabrication.

Room corner treatment adds 25mm curved plaster fillets at convex perimeter wall intersections in the photo copy. It retains translucent-wall visibility, leaves concave recess corners intact and omits intersections adjacent to opening voids. Wall panel subdivisions are not rounded separately, avoiding seams around window/door decomposition. Free-standing partitions without a joined perimeter intersection retain their existing geometry.

The photo pass renders a progressively sampled PNG at 1600px by default (1000/2400px options), with the captured camera aspect. Lighting is isolated from the interactive studio lights. Its canvas temporarily overlays the live canvas **in the same viewport**. After capture, a 220ms white shutter flash plays, photo GPU resources are disposed, and the live canvas is exposed again. Reduced-motion preferences suppress the flash animation. The PNG then opens in a native modal dialog with an image preview, Download PNG link and Close/Escape handling. Blob URLs live until the dialog closes and are revoked on close/unmount. Capture errors restore the live view and show retry feedback; repeated clicks are blocked during a capture.

## Opening lighting and global illumination

The photo-only pipeline dynamically loads `three-gpu-pathtracer` 0.0.23 with `three-mesh-bvh` 0.8.3 on the existing Three.js 0.172 renderer. It traces the captured, eased geometry, original camera projection/poses, material groups and metre/inch grain UVs. The tracing view prunes hidden ancestors because the upstream scene generator inspects child visibility independently. The interactive renderer does not import or instantiate the tracer and retains its studio lights.

Viewport lights are replaced in the private photo scene by one-sided rectangular sources sized to each actual opening, facing into the room from 40mm outside the aperture. Perimeter windows use 6500K and intensity 100; doors and other openings use 3000K and intensity 12. Temperatures use approximate black-body RGB converted to linear color. Intensity is emitter luminance in Three.js units, a configurable preview assumption rather than a measurement of a specific site. The source represents diffuse exterior sky or adjacent-room illumination; it does not simulate location/time-specific sun or sky. Existing frames, translucent panes and opaque closed door leaves remain authoritative occluders. A closed solid door therefore transmits little adjacent light. Partition apertures receive opposing sources for their two sides; these use the same per-opening settings.

`PhotoSettings` exposes daylight and adjacent defaults plus per-opening ID overrides (temperature/intensity), bounce count, sample count, denoising, image size, exposure and tone mapping. Zero intensity disables a source. Settings are validated and copied before asynchronous work. Visible photo controls adjust the two light groups and quality/image size and a Reduce photo noise toggle; per-opening overrides remain available through the render API. Settings are session-local and do not change saved room geometry or protocol.

Multiple importance sampling integrates area sources with up to six path steps by default, including indirect diffuse color transfer and PBR glossy reflections across walls, floors, countertops, cabinetry and other visible surfaces. There is no invented ambient/GI fill and no environment illumination leaking through the room enclosure. A room without illuminated apertures will render dark. The existing translucent-wall cutaway and window opacity remain unchanged, so transport reflects those display materials rather than treating walls as opaque architectural plaster.

The pinned photo adapter uses stratified sampling with a fixed 64x64 void-and-cluster blue-noise rank texture (generated deterministically with seed 1492026 using the pinned tracer's BlueNoiseGenerator). Stable noise resets the stratified sampler and seed; a fixed 256 completed samples makes results repeatable on the same GPU/browser independent of frame timing. Cross-device floating-point pixels need not be identical. Quick uses 64 samples; Fine uses 512. Six steps is bounded GI, not an unlimited-bounce solution. Glossy filtering at 0.5 suppresses high-variance indirect specular paths at the cost of sharp caustics. ACES filmic output with exposure 1 is the default; the render options keep exposure/tone mapping extensible without changing the captured camera.

After accumulation, a deterministic photo-only denoiser runs three 25-tap à-trous passes at pixel spacing 1, 2 and 4. Temporary normal/depth and base-color guides come from the same visible scene, poses, camera, material groups and UV maps. Geometry normals (without texture normal maps) and relative view-space depth constrain smoothing across cabinet edges; base-color differences further separate surfaces. Filtering operates on lighting divided by base color, then reapplies the original per-pixel base color, retaining grain and painted-material details. Tone mapping and display color conversion apply once, in the final pass. Background pixels and alpha are preserved. All temporary targets and guide materials are released on success/failure without disposing captured geometry or shared texture maps. The filter may soften fine specular/shadow details, especially in Quick mode; it can be disabled for raw output. The center sample always retains a positive weight; missing/degenerate guide normals skip neighbor filtering and preserve the original lighting instead of producing black surfaces. Texture normal maps remain authoritative in the path tracer, but are excluded from the geometry guide so microdetail and degenerate tangent frames cannot invalidate it. Transparency makes the guide approximation imperfect; this is a bounded spatial filter, not a neural denoiser.

A 3x3 tile layout batches small tiles for up to 12ms before yielding (one expensive tile may exceed that budget) and reports progress in the existing viewport. Work grows approximately with pixel count, samples and path length; 2400px needs about 2.25 times the pixel work of 1600px, and Fine twice Standard's samples. Expect seconds to minutes depending on GPU, material texture count and scene complexity; these are expectations, not measured production benchmarks. BVH/texture preparation and first shader compilation add startup cost, and preparation is synchronous. Cancel/unmount abort between tiles; context loss and a three-minute sampling limit report retryable errors and dispose the photo context. WebGL2 floating-point color buffers are required; unsupported devices report an error instead of silently producing a raster image without GI. Resources for the tracer, captured scene and canvas are disposed on success and failure. The pinned adapter also repairs the upstream 0.0.23 renamed-quad disposal bug and releases its otherwise omitted low-resolution target/material and generated geometry.

## Validation

Tests cover material definition validity, asset integrity, saved-room compatibility and rejection, room/countertop transitions with identical positions and cutouts, cached texture lifetime during repeated drags, camera/pose/visibility fidelity, independent snapshot materials, stock bounds, countertop holes, custom front groups/grain, photo-only wall fillets and transparency, same-viewport render/flash/cleanup, and PNG preview/download/Escape URL lifetime. Run the repository's `npm run verify` and pre-push hook before publishing a PR.

A Chrome GPU smoke scene (512px, 32 samples, six steps, rectangular daylight emitter, white floor/cube and red wall) rendered in 6.7 seconds initially and 5.1 seconds on repetition, with identical PNG SHA-256 hashes across both captures. This earlier raw PCG capture validates the initial shader execution and same-device determinism; it is not a full-room production timing benchmark.

The noise-reduction revision is also checked in real Chrome GPU renders, including raw/filtered comparison and a textured reference cabinet room. Regression tests cover fixed rank distribution, guide resource ownership, source fidelity and cleanup after GPU failure.

At equal 96-sample/512px settings, the guided pass reduced adjacent-pixel RGB RMS variation in a smooth red-wall patch from 6.25 to 0.54 (about 91 percent), while visually retaining the cube silhouette and red-wall indirect illumination. This is a representative patch measurement, not a guarantee for every material or lighting configuration.
