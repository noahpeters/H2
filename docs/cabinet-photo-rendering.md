# Room surfaces and Take Photo

Room controls select three wall treatments (warm plaster, white plaster, sage painted plaster), three floor treatments (light wood planks, dark stained planks, smooth concrete), and three countertop treatments (white quartz, Taj Mahal, dark granite). Existing `room.walls` and `room.floor` IDs are retained, including legacy oak/walnut IDs; the plank scan does not identify its wood species. Optional `room.countertopMaterial` persists through existing room JSON save/load, undo/redo and sharing; older rooms default to white quartz. Server validation rejects unknown countertop IDs without changing the room protocol version.

`roomMaterials.ts` uses the existing `MaterialDefinition`, `createMaterial`, immutable texture cache, load/fallback handling and metre-based UV projection. Geometry factories still produce the same stock, openings, overhangs and sink cutouts. `StudyScene` assigns room PBR materials to floors, walls, cabinet countertops, dishwasher countertops and island tops. Cache signatures include countertop selection. Walls retain their existing transparency and floors retain double-sided rendering. Material transitions wait for texture loading and preserve the previous scene until replacement is ready.

## Photo lifecycle

The interactive Three.js renderer remains mounted and demand-rendered with its existing camera, controls, geometry and renderer configuration. `Take Photo` synchronously captures a private scene/camera copy, including current door/drawer poses, transforms, visibility, source FOV, aspect, zoom, orbit target, material definitions and grain mapping. Capture waits until the live scene has finished its current material transition. Geometry and materials belong to the copy; texture resources are retained through the existing reference-counted cache. Later design edits and camera navigation cannot modify captured pixels.

`photoRender.ts` is a separate on-demand Three.js render pipeline and GPU lifetime. It eases rectangular cabinet stock by 1mm and countertops by 2mm. Box envelopes stay exact; extrusions inset their end caps and retain their central outline, thickness and holes. Shaker/slatted custom front pieces retain independent grain axes and glass material groups. Profile-deformed stock is kept intact rather than replaced by rectangular approximations. Unsupported geometry shapes retain their exact current mesh; they can acquire additional photo treatments later. All enhancement geometry is created in the photo copy and never enters saved designs, placement, dimensions, snapping, takeoffs or fabrication.

Room corner treatment adds 25mm curved plaster fillets at convex perimeter wall intersections in the photo copy. It retains translucent-wall visibility, leaves concave recess corners intact and omits intersections adjacent to opening voids. Wall panel subdivisions are not rounded separately, avoiding seams around window/door decomposition. Free-standing partitions without a joined perimeter intersection retain their existing geometry.

The photo pass renders a progressively sampled PNG at 1600px by default (1000/2400px options), with the captured camera aspect and the architectural framing described below. Lighting is isolated from the interactive studio lights. Its canvas temporarily overlays the live canvas **in the same viewport**. After capture, a 220ms white shutter flash plays, photo GPU resources are disposed, and the live canvas is exposed again. Reduced-motion preferences suppress the flash animation. The PNG then opens in a native modal dialog with an image preview, Download PNG link and Close/Escape handling. Blob URLs live until the dialog closes and are revoked on close/unmount. Capture errors restore the live view and show retry feedback; repeated clicks are blocked during a capture.

## Opening lighting and global illumination

The photo-only pipeline dynamically loads `three-gpu-pathtracer` 0.0.23 with `three-mesh-bvh` 0.8.3 on the existing Three.js 0.172 renderer. It traces the captured, eased geometry, original camera projection/poses, material groups and metre/inch grain UVs. The tracing view prunes hidden ancestors because the upstream scene generator inspects child visibility independently. The interactive renderer does not import or instantiate the tracer and retains its studio lights.

Viewport lights are replaced in the private photo scene by one-sided rectangular sources sized to each actual opening, facing into the room from 40mm outside the aperture. Perimeter windows use 6500K and intensity 100; doors and other openings use 3000K and intensity 12. Temperatures use approximate black-body RGB converted to linear color. Intensity is emitter luminance in Three.js units, a configurable preview assumption rather than a measurement of a specific site. The source represents diffuse exterior sky or adjacent-room illumination; it does not simulate location/time-specific sun or sky. Existing frames, translucent panes and opaque closed door leaves remain authoritative occluders. A closed solid door therefore transmits little adjacent light. Partition apertures receive opposing sources for their two sides; these use the same per-opening settings.

`PhotoSettings` exposes daylight and adjacent defaults plus per-opening ID overrides (temperature/intensity), bounce count, sample count, denoising, image size, exposure and tone mapping. Zero intensity disables a source. Settings are validated and copied before asynchronous work. Visible photo controls adjust the two light groups and quality/image size and a Reduce photo noise toggle; per-opening overrides remain available through the render API. Settings are session-local and do not change saved room geometry or protocol.

Multiple importance sampling integrates area sources with up to six path steps by default, including indirect diffuse color transfer and PBR glossy reflections across walls, floors, countertops, cabinetry and other visible surfaces. There is no invented ambient/GI fill and no environment illumination leaking through the room enclosure. A room without illuminated apertures will render dark. Inside-camera photos use opaque walls; outside-camera photos hide the near cutaway walls and their opening assemblies. Opening light sources remain active across those camera cutaways, with the same temperatures, intensities and per-opening overrides. Visible frames, panes and closed doors still occlude their sources; cutaway assemblies are absent from transport. Other intentionally hidden ancestors remain excluded from lighting. The live viewport retains translucent walls and window glass retains its existing opacity.

The pinned photo adapter uses stratified sampling with a fixed 64x64 void-and-cluster blue-noise rank texture (generated deterministically with seed 1492026 using the pinned tracer's BlueNoiseGenerator). Stable noise resets the stratified sampler and seed; a fixed 256 completed samples makes results repeatable on the same GPU/browser independent of frame timing. Cross-device floating-point pixels need not be identical. Quick uses 64 samples; Fine uses 512. Six steps is bounded GI, not an unlimited-bounce solution. Glossy filtering at 0.5 suppresses high-variance indirect specular paths at the cost of sharp caustics. AgX with automatic exposure is now the default; milestone 7 adds a separate linear-HDR finishing pass and deterministic architectural framing (see below).

After accumulation, a deterministic photo-only denoiser runs three 25-tap à-trous passes at pixel spacing 1, 2 and 4. Temporary normal/depth and base-color guides come from the same visible scene, poses, camera, material groups and UV maps. Geometry normals (without texture normal maps) and relative view-space depth constrain smoothing across cabinet edges; base-color differences further separate surfaces. Filtering operates on lighting divided by base color, then reapplies the original per-pixel base color, retaining grain and painted-material details. Tone mapping and display color conversion apply once, in the final pass. Background pixels and alpha are preserved. All temporary targets and guide materials are released on success/failure without disposing captured geometry or shared texture maps. The filter may soften fine specular/shadow details, especially in Quick mode; it can be disabled for raw output. The center sample always retains a positive weight; missing/degenerate guide normals skip neighbor filtering and preserve the original lighting instead of producing black surfaces. Texture normal maps remain authoritative in the path tracer, but are excluded from the geometry guide so microdetail and degenerate tangent frames cannot invalidate it. Transparency makes the guide approximation imperfect; this is a bounded spatial filter, not a neural denoiser.

A 3x3 tile layout batches small tiles for up to 12ms before yielding (one expensive tile may exceed that budget) and reports progress in the existing viewport. Work grows approximately with pixel count, samples and path length; 2400px needs about 2.25 times the pixel work of 1600px, with quality supersampling adding the pixel-area costs described below. Expect seconds to minutes depending on GPU, material texture count and scene complexity; these are expectations, not measured production benchmarks. BVH/texture preparation and first shader compilation add startup cost, and preparation is synchronous. Cancel/unmount abort between tiles; context loss and a resolution/sample-scaled sampling limit (three to fifteen minutes) report retryable errors and dispose the photo context. WebGL2 floating-point color buffers are required; unsupported devices report an error instead of silently producing a raster image without GI. Resources for the tracer, captured scene and canvas are disposed on success and failure. The pinned adapter also repairs the upstream 0.0.23 renamed-quad disposal bug and releases its otherwise omitted low-resolution target/material and generated geometry.

## Validation

Tests cover material definition validity, asset integrity, saved-room compatibility and rejection, room/countertop transitions with identical positions and cutouts, cached texture lifetime during repeated drags, camera/pose/visibility fidelity, independent snapshot materials, stock bounds, countertop holes, custom front groups/grain, photo-only wall fillets and transparency, same-viewport render/flash/cleanup, and PNG preview/download/Escape URL lifetime. Run the repository's `npm run verify` and pre-push hook before publishing a PR.

A Chrome GPU smoke scene (512px, 32 samples, six steps, rectangular daylight emitter, white floor/cube and red wall) rendered in 6.7 seconds initially and 5.1 seconds on repetition, with identical PNG SHA-256 hashes across both captures. This earlier raw PCG capture validates the initial shader execution and same-device determinism; it is not a full-room production timing benchmark.

The noise-reduction revision is also checked in real Chrome GPU renders, including raw/filtered comparison and a textured reference cabinet room. Regression tests cover fixed rank distribution, guide resource ownership, source fidelity and cleanup after GPU failure.

At equal 96-sample/512px settings, the guided pass reduced adjacent-pixel RGB RMS variation in a smooth red-wall patch from 6.25 to 0.54 (about 91 percent), while visually retaining the cube silhouette and red-wall indirect illumination. This is a representative patch measurement, not a guarantee for every material or lighting configuration.

## Ambient occlusion and contact detail (milestone 6)

Take Photo uses **ray-traced contact visibility and multi-bounce occlusion**, rather than a screen-space AO multiply. GTAO/HBAO would lose off-screen occluders and darken indirect illumination a second time in this full path-tracing stack. The photo-only pinned shader adapter instead increases complete, independent transport paths at pixels near geometry contacts. Each path retains the existing BSDF, area-light shadow/transparency tests, multiple importance sampling, material emission, bounce budget and indirect color transfer. Their radiance and alpha are averaged before the existing tone mapping. There is no AO tint, cavity mask applied to color, ambient fill, or material/geometry/camera mutation.

Eight fixed cosine-distributed hemisphere probes at the primary geometric hit detect nearby surfaces in world metres, including grazing directions for floor contacts and reveals. They allocate sampling work only; they never determine pixel darkness, and translucent probe hits only increase work (actual transport still uses the original transparency). Probe directions consume no random samples, and all refinement paths reuse the exact same jittered camera ray with successive PCG draws in the original fixture and distinct fixed pixel-rank offsets in the current stratified sampler. Each refined stratified path resets its bounce-row index so it stays within the upstream one-path sample texture. The bounded full-path loop preserves same-device capture determinism. Derived tracing meshes receive traversal-stable IDs because the upstream BVH builder sorts random clone UUIDs, which otherwise changes coplanar-contact tie ordering between captures. Snapshot and live object identities remain unchanged. A conservative position-scaled 0.01mm base ray offset replaces the upstream 0.1mm offset to reduce missed sub-mm hardware contacts and light leakage. Large world positions still increase the offset for floating-point stability.

`PhotoSettings.contacts` and visible photo controls expose quality (`off`, `standard`, `fine`), intensity/strength (0–1) and radius (1–200mm). Defaults are **Standard, strength 1, radius 50mm**: two complete paths near detected contacts; Fine uses four. Strength controls additional sampling effort, quantized to whole paths, rather than inventing extra darkness. Off/zero uses the base photo sampling and retains physically traced occlusion. Omitted contact options use the default for backwards-compatible render API callers. Radius controls where refinement is spent, never the physical reach of GI or shadows. Settings remain session-local and are copied before rendering.

Targets are cabinet/floor grounding, countertop supports, door/drawer/face-frame gaps, toe kicks, wall/cabinet junctions, hardware mounting feet and inside corners. Existing geometry must actually contain a gap or intersection to produce it: no fabricated seams or grunge. Contacts with little illumination stay dark according to light transport; increasing contact strength should primarily improve convergence, not lower the mean brightness. Broad open surfaces remain at one path. The eight detection probes cost BVH intersections per camera sample; detected contacts cost about 2x/4x transport at Standard/Fine. Total time depends on contact coverage and scene complexity; there is no additional G-buffer or screen-size AO render target. Base output sample counts/progress and the current quality-scaled three-to-fifteen-minute time limit remain in force. Very fine details below output pixel resolution still require a larger image; eight probes can miss small isolated neighbours, falling back to ordinary physically correct sampling.

Tests exercise the real pinned shader, preservation of GI/MIS expressions, averaging, default/disabled/fine effort and invalid settings, alongside existing snapshot fidelity and photo lifecycle tests. GPU smoke checks should cover floor contact, tight fronts, countertop support and repeat hashes in addition to shader compilation.

A Chrome GPU contact fixture (512px, 32 base samples, six steps, cabinet-sized floor contact, thin fronts and countertop support) compiled and rendered without console errors. Two Standard captures took 5.075/5.144 seconds and produced the same PNG SHA-256 (`ea0ae2ada37cea91273bb54655ec40ed4e185bbca83c0c00d413e411931628d5`). This is a small fixture, not a production-room timing benchmark or exhaustive visual acceptance of every target; 32 samples still show indirect-light noise.

## Architectural camera and photographic finishing (milestone 7)

Take Photo now uses AgX from the pinned Three.js r172 release. Path-traced radiance and the existing normal/depth/albedo-guided denoiser stay linear HDR; exposure, global white balance, area reconstruction and restrained bright-source bloom precede the single AgX/sRGB display transform. Both noise-reduced and unfiltered photos use this same finishing pass. Existing room materials, grain, easing, opening-light temperatures, GI and contact detail are retained.

The default is a 28mm lens on a 36mm-wide sensor cropped to the captured aspect, with zero additional vertical shift. Architectural correction levels pitch and roll, retaining captured position, horizontal heading and zoom. It derives the off-axis frustum so the captured orbit target retains its image height, then adds the requested shift (positive frames higher). The inverse projection is updated for the tracer and denoiser. Geometry, material assignments and visibility are never changed by correction. Lens/correction intentionally changes the field of view; disable **Keep vertical lines straight** to retain the exact captured camera including FOV and projection. Near-vertical overhead views require selecting a room-level view when correction is enabled. There is no depth of field: the ordinary PerspectiveCamera disables the tracer's aperture sampling.

**Auto Exposure** defaults on. A deterministic 64×64 sample of captured HDR radiance measures median luminance, targeting 0.35, with a p98 highlight limit of 8 and a bounded 1/16–16 multiplier. This protects practical window highlights while balancing the occupied room; it cannot recover an exterior scene absent from the lighting model or guarantee detail in every extreme source. With auto off, the existing exposure multiplier (default 1) is used. **Exposure Compensation** is ±4EV in quarter-stop steps and works in either mode. Invalid/negative radiance is excluded; dark/no-data captures have a finite fallback and do not invent lighting.

**Auto White Balance** defaults on. A small unlit albedo guide identifies near-neutral surfaces (at least 0.2 linear brightness, channel spread at most 8%); colored wood/paint, extreme HDR highlights and dark pixels cannot define white. At least eight valid neutral samples are required. One global RGB adaptation, bounded to 0.8–1.25, preserves differences between warm adjacent-room and cool daylight illumination. With insufficient evidence, correction stays neutral. Turning auto off enables **Temperature override**, 2000–10000K, default 6500K, using the existing approximate black-body color model relative to 6500K. No light-source colors or material colors are rewritten.

**Photo Quality** controls sampling and supersampling together: Quick = 64 samples at 1× output resolution; Standard (default) = 256 at 1.5×; Fine = 512 at 2×. Output defaults to 1600px on the longer side, with 1000/2400px choices and the captured aspect. A deterministic 4×4 area resolve integrates the larger HDR image before tone mapping. At equal output size, Standard traces roughly 9× Quick's pixel-samples and Fine roughly 3.56× Standard's; denoiser memory scales with render pixel area (2.25× / 4×). The device texture/renderbuffer limit is checked with actionable feedback rather than silently reducing quality. GPU failures, cancellation and timeout still dispose the private photo resources and restore the live view. Hardware/runtime differences mean these ratios are work estimates, not time guarantees.

Bloom is a fixed local 25-tap bright-source spread, contributing only 0.8%, with a soft exposed-HDR threshold of 4–8 and a capped source contribution of 16. Ordinary surfaces contribute none; no vignette, grain, chromatic aberration, stylized effects or background blur is added. The full room remains sharp. Camera settings are a validated JSON-serializable value under `PhotoSettings.camera`, captured before yielding. Older callers missing that field use camera defaults and retain their sample count; UI settings currently belong to the viewport session, separate from saved design geometry.

Validation includes camera projection/target invariants, portrait/landscape sensor behavior, metering/highlight guards, WB colored-surface exclusion, settings validation, finishing resource ownership and existing scene/denoiser tests. Serve `scripts/fixtures/photo-finishing.html` with Vite for real WebGL regression of HDR highlight separation through AgX, both directly and through linear denoising. The fixture checks shader compilation, pixel values and GPU errors.
